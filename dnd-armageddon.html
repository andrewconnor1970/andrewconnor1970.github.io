The Ninth Hell dossier

Asmodeus, Nessus, and the Future Armageddon

A source-critical Dungeons & Dragons lore archive separating official statements, contradictory traditions, published uncertainty, and clearly labelled synthesis.

Scope: D&D game lore only. This research does not equate D&D cosmology with real-world religion or mythology.

The short answer

The remembered event is real D&D lore, but not a continuously maintained metaplot. Its clearest form appears in Chris Pramas's 1999 AD&D Second Edition sourcebook Guide to Hell . In that account, Asmodeus is secretly Ahriman, one of the primordial Serpents of Law. Unbelieving souls nourish the wounds of the ancient fall, and full recovery would allow Ahriman to withdraw the lawful power holding the planes together, survive the resulting Armageddon, and rebuild existence without Jazirian.

Later sources preserve important pieces without always naming the complete event. Fiendish Codex II retains the hidden Nessian Guard and describes a future conflict more apocalyptic than the Blood War. Chains of Asmodeus restores the Ahriman and Jazirian tradition, the wounded serpent, the unbeliever-soul mechanism, deliberate cultivation of disbelief, ten avatars, and Malsheem.

What the audit establishes

  • The complete named Armageddon belongs primarily to Guide to Hell .
  • Asmodeus's blood creates a concealed elite host in Nessus, outside the Blood War order of battle.
  • The Blood War can function as camouflage and political theatre rather than a war Asmodeus must first win.
  • The Bringing is a separate Planescape-era rumor. Connecting it to Armageddon remains a useful but unconfirmed reconciliation.
  • No published source supplies a date, complete order of battle, or fixed adventure-path resolution.

Why Nessus matters

Nessus functions as supreme command center, recovery site, sealed reserve, strategic archive, and possible launch point for the end of the current planar order. Malsheem and the Serpent's Coil sit at the heart of a layer that other archdevils cannot freely inspect.

Reliability framework

Grade A: directly stated in a named TSR or Wizards source. Grade B: preserved by a later official source with a changed explanation. Grade C: presented as rumor, myth, unreliable testimony, or theory. Grade D: this archive's clearly labelled campaign synthesis.
